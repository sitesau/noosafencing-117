Boundary and neighbour sequence

For a sufficient dividing fence, adjoining owners should begin with a written description of the proposed work and at least one quote. Keep the scope clear enough for a neighbour conversation: run, height, material, removal, gate allowances and exclusions. A sufficient fence is not the same as an upgraded preference, so extra height, premium finish or a preferred style may not be automatically shared when one owner wants more than the sufficient outcome.

If agreement is not reached, the formal step is a notice to contribute. The pathway allows one month to reach agreement after the notice, then a possible Queensland Civil and Administrative Tribunal application within two months of the notice. Match material to the job

Read service categories as a decision path, not a menu. A privacy boundary, street presentation fence, pool barrier and paddock boundary solve different problems, so one rate cannot explain them properly. Coastal residential work points toward Colorbond, aluminium slat fencing and screens, with attention to wind, salt air and front fence presentation. Established hinterland streets and shared dividing fences remain relevant for timber, including paling, lapped and capped, and picket options.

Rural work is separate again: ringlock, star picket and strained wire fencing suit acreage boundaries around Doonan, Verrierdale, Cooran and Kin Kin. Pool fencing is compliance led, built to the Queensland pool safety standard, with barrier requirements and inspection pathways confirmed by a licensed pool safety inspector rather than assumed from a standard boundary enquiry.

Read the quote against the site

A low metre figure can mislead when the site is awkward. A rate can help frame a rough project range, but it does not describe coastal sand, riverfront alluvium or hinterland clay, and those ground types dig and hold posts differently. Check footing assumptions, not just the finished fence face. Confirm removal and disposal of the old fence, access limits, corners, returns and gate openings. Compare the proposed height against ground levels and any retaining wall, and ask how corner block sight line treatment affects the front or side fence.

  • Separate materials, labour, footings, removal and exclusions in writing.
  • Ask whether access limits have been priced or only noted.
  • For pool areas, list what must be checked by a licensed pool safety inspector.

  2. Choose by purpose. Decide whether the fence is mainly for privacy, airflow, street presentation, pool safety, acreage control or access.
  3. Raise compliance early. Ask about QBCC licensing where required and use a licensed pool safety inspector for pool barrier confirmation.

Common questions

What should be written down before approaching a neighbour about a dividing fence? Prepare a written description of the proposed work and at least one quote. Include the run, height, material, removal, gate allowances and exclusions, so the discussion can separate a sufficient fence from upgraded preferences.

Why can two fencing quotes for the same length be far apart? Metre rates do not capture coastal sand, riverfront alluvium or hinterland clay, and they may skip access limits, corners, returns, gate openings, old fence removal, disposal and retaining or sight line adjustments.

Is pool fencing handled like a normal boundary fence quote? No. Pool fencing is compliance led under the Queensland pool safety standard, and barrier requirements plus inspection pathways are confirmed by a licensed pool safety inspector, even when the work sits near other fencing.
